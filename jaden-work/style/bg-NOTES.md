# Background: notes for the director

Runtime data: `resources/jaden/bg.style.js`, which sets `window.JADEN.bg`. It's generated by `jaden-work/tools/bg/build_bg.py` (with `hand.py` for the hand and `bg_runtime.js` for the helpers), so edit the builder and rerun it with `jaden-work/venv/bin/python jaden-work/tools/bg/build_bg.py`. The preview is `review/bg.html`, which has live controls, a sketch-in player, the dusk-wash player, the framings and a hand check. Static exports are `review/png/bg-*.png`.

Every element is an ordinary pencil figure, `{strokes, style}`, in Jasper's units (100 = one ruled line), so `Pencil.render` draws it like the box, including `seed`, `reg`, `color`, `build`, `groups`, `alpha` and `only`.

## World convention
The figures stand on **y = 0**, centred on **x = 0**. The authored world spans x ±2650 and y −2000…+200. The horizon sits at **y ≈ −215**, about a third of the way up the box. It is drawn in two halves that stop about 460 units either side of x = 0, so they never cross a figure. A hidden stroke, `hz-mid`, fills the gap; use `show: ['hz-mid']` on the horizon only if the stage is ever empty. A faint blue ground line at y = 0 continues the box's own guide.

## `window.JADEN.bg`
| Key | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `elements.{sky, sun, cloudA, cloudB, hillL, hillR, horizon, tree, palm, grass}` | Golden-hour figures. |
| `dusk.{wash, sun, cloudA, cloudB, hillL, hillR}` | Dusk figures. `dusk.X` shares the **frame and placement** of `elements.X`; `dusk.wash` is in world units. |
| `layout.place[el]` | World position of each element's anchor in the wide framing. Render with `x: ox + px*s, y: oy + py*s, scale: s`. |
| `layout.tall` | Overrides for portrait (aspect below about 0.8): the sun and `cloudB` move into the phone column above the box. |
| `layout.sunset` (`wide`, `tall`) | The sun's held key positions as it sets: `[fraction of the wash, x, y]`. Use `bg.sunAt(k, tall)`. `duskPlace` and `tallDuskPlace` are the last keys. |
| `layout.order` | Back-to-front order. Everything multiplies, so the order only matters for knockouts (see below). |
| `layout.color` | Use 0.64, the figures' resting value. The background is already lighter in the data: thinner lines (4–5 units, pressure ~0.6 versus Jasper's 7 at ~0.8) and marker opacity 0.45–0.8. |
| `layout.sketchIn`, `layout.duskBeats` | Staging; see below. |
| `palette` | Shared by every figure (`style.palette` is the same object). |
| `fig(name, set)`, `place(name, overrides)` | Small lookups. |
| `sketchAt(phase, ms)` | `{el: build or null}` for the sketch-in. |
| `duskState(k)` | The dusk at wash drawing `k`: `S.opts[el]` holds the render options (accent progress); `S[el]` is groups only. |
| `sunAt(k, tall)` | The sun's position at wash drawing `k` (held keys). |
| `merge(items)` | Merges elements into one placed figure, so a frame costs one render. |

Groups per element: one per tuft and dash (`tuftA`…`tuftF`, `dash1`…`dash3`), one per sky leg (`sky-a1`…), and `cloudA`, `sun` and so on. Each has a pivot, so the director can shift a tuft or move a cloud aside (v1 blew a cloud off the greeting) by group transform.

## How each element is drawn
The line is graphite in a lighter hand than Jasper's, lit side lighter and shadow side heavier. Construction is sparse blue: horizon and ground guides, the sun's centre cross, the cloud base lines. Each of these has the full genga stack (`build` works layer by layer):
- **sun**: one loop with an imperfect closure and a searching pass at the lower right; three quick yellow marker passes at varied angles and a warm touch at the bottom. The upper left is left as paper, so it reads as a sun, not a ball.
- **clouds**: anime cumulus with irregular scallops; a red kage boundary, a lavender kage and sparse red hatching. The body stays paper. `occluders` is the body, for knockout.
- **hills**: the colour is a band under the ridge only, deeper on the shadow flank and stopped early on the lit side, with a red boundary and a little hatching. `hillR` is the farther, bluer one.
- **trees**: a small round tree on `hillL` and a small **palm** on `hillR`, a quiet Vice City nod.
- **grass**: four tufts flanking the figures, two small far ones and three ground dashes, each with a green marker dab.
- **sky (golden)**: two small patches of overlapping light-blue marker legs, one framing the top of `cloudA` and one over `cloudB`. The rest of the sky is paper.

## Sketch-in (with `build`)
`bg.sketchAt(phase, ms)` returns per-element `build` objects. `null` means not started, so skip that render; `{}` means complete. There are two phases around the box's own write-on:
1. `'roughs'`, about 1.7 s: the blue horizon guides, then the horizon, then `hillL` and `hillR` lines, quick and light.
2. `'details'`, about 4.6 s, after the box has its colour: the sun, the clouds, the tree, the palm and the grass as lines; then colour on the sun, hills, trees and sky legs; then shadows (kage, red lines, hatching) and the grass dabs.

Quantise `ms` to the drawing rate (2s or 3s) and give each drawing a fresh `seed` and a small `reg`. The `ms` values in `layout.sketchIn` are only suggestions, so retime freely. A tap fast-forwards the sketch-in: call `sketchAt('details', Infinity)`.

## Dusk wash (the reveal), revised after animation QA round 1 (B1, B2)
`dusk.wash` is now **12 zig-zag marker passes** with 3–4 legs each, so each colour zone gets 13–15 legs at ±10°:
- gold/orange low, around the sun and over the hills;
- pink in the middle;
- violet and indigo high and sparse.

Most of the sky stays paper. Each leg is a Pencil **accent**: one tooth-textured coloured-pencil/marker path, multiplied. Legs are spaced a little less than a nib apart, so each shows a darker seam where it overlaps the last, and different passes build up darker where they cross. The tints (`palette.dusk*`) are light, as they should read on paper. The dusk touches (cloud undersides, sun, hills) are accents in the same way, in their elements' frames.

**`duskState(k)` (API change, backward compatible).** `layout.duskBeats` is 40 drawings on 3s, about 5 s. Each beat is `[[el, pass, progress]]`, so a pass is **drawn on over 2–3 drawings** (progress 0.3, 0.68, 1 or 0.55, 1). `S = bg.duskState(k)` returns:
- `S.opts[el]` = `{accent, accentAmount, groups}`, the full render options for that drawing. **Use these.**
- `S[el]` is groups only, as before: passes not yet started are hidden. On its own it no longer draws the wash, because accents need `accent`/`accentAmount`.
- `S.progress[el]` = `[[pass, 0..1], …]`.

```js
const S = bg.duskState(k);
Pencil.render(g, bg.dusk.wash, Object.assign({seed, color}, S.opts.wash));                 // at world 0,0
for (const el of ['cloudA', 'cloudB', 'hillL', 'hillR']) Pencil.render(g, bg.dusk[el], Object.assign({seed, color, ...placement(el)}, S.opts[el]));
```

**The sun sets in held keys (B2).** Use `bg.sunAt(k, tall)` for the sun's position at wash drawing `k`:
- 5 keys (`layout.sunset.wide` / `.tall`), held between moves;
- each move is one inbetween drawing a little past halfway, then the key;
- the wide keys end half behind `hillL`; the tall keys start from the director's phone reveal position (−640, −800) and sink to (−690, −300).

**Knockout.** `hillL`, `hillR` and `horizon` now have land `occluders`, so render them with `layers: {knockout: true}`; this is harmless when no sun is behind them. Render `dusk.sun` (with `S.opts.sun`) **right after `elements.sun`, before the land**, so the hills hide it too. Over the wash, keep the clouds knocked out as before.

The three changes to `drawBgFigure` I tested in a copy of the page (`qa/b1/sheet_*-after.png`):
1. In `bgPlace`: `if (name === 'sun' && sunset > 0) p = BGJ.sunAt(sunset * DUSK_N, tall);`
2. In the element loop: add `layers: name.startsWith('hill') || name === 'horizon' ? {knockout: true} : undefined`; after the sun, add `if (S && name === 'sun') put('sun', BGJ.dusk.sun, S.opts.sun);`
3. Wash and touches: use `S.opts.wash` / `S.opts[name]` instead of `{groups: S[name]}`, and skip `sun` there.

The wash is not cut around the handwriting. Clip it away from the greeting as planned.

**Data (B3).** Coordinates are whole units. Pen widths keep 0.1 and weights 0.01.

## Performance
In headless Chrome (GPU on) at 2880 × 1800, each element costs about 2–4 ms. The whole golden background is about 27 ms, and about 33 ms with the wash. Every medium multiplies, so the background is order-independent apart from the knockouts. That means you can:
- render the finished background **once per boil seed** into 2–3 offscreen canvases and blit them;
- during the sketch-in, re-render only the elements that are changing, over a cached composite of the finished ones;
- during the wash, cache the golden background, then multiply on the wash and accents, and re-render the clouds with knockout on top.

`bg.merge([...])` combines elements into one figure, which saves a little: 90 → 72 ms for the dusk set with the GPU off. Call `Pencil.warm(fig, {scale})` for each element during the box write-on.

## Weak spots
- **The texture is pencil-tooth on a marker tint.** At high zoom it can read as soft pastel rather than marker. The accent `level` (0.72) sets how much tooth shows.
- **The hill dusk passes read as cool grey-violet** over the sage, not pink-violet. That is deliberate (an evening shadow), but it is easy to recolour (`palette.duskHill`).
- **The central pink pass (`p07`) sits behind the greeting** on both laptop and phone. The director's clip should take it out. If that leaves the middle too bare, move its start in `WASH`.
- **No renderer changes were needed.** Red kage boundaries use `shadow.edge`, so they build in genga order. Edge weight is fixed in the renderer, so they match the figures' red weight rather than being lighter.
